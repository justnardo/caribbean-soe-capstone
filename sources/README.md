# `sources/` — the shareable folder

This folder is the **`_CAN-SHARE/`** side of the two-folder discipline described in `09-Ethics-Access-and-Sharing.md`. Everything in here can be handed to anyone — a supervisor, a classmate, an ethics reviewer — without a licensing or confidentiality problem.

## What's in here

### `OECD-2024-Guidelines-SOEs-CC-BY-4.0.pdf`

Your own copy of the benchmark. 82 pages, 2,010,570 bytes.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cite as** | OECD (2024), *OECD Guidelines on Corporate Governance of State-Owned Enterprises 2024*, OECD Publishing, Paris |
| **DOI** | `https://doi.org/10.1787/18a24f43-en` |
| **Licence** | **CC BY 4.0** — you may reproduce, redistribute and adapt it, including commercially, **provided you attribute it**. |
| **ISBN (PDF)** | 978-92-64-56907-2 |

**Under CC BY 4.0 you may:**
- ✅ Attach this PDF to your submission
- ✅ Reproduce its tables, figures and extended quotations in your dissertation
- ✅ Share it with classmates, your supervisor, or post it in your course materials
- ✅ Extract text from it for your coding (as this pack has done)

**The one condition:** attribute it. In an academic dissertation a full Harvard reference to the OECD *is* the attribution — you are satisfying the licence and the citation convention with the same act. If you reproduce a table or figure, add "Source: OECD (2024)" beneath it. If you ever adapt the material, note that you have changed it.

> **Two cautions that matter more than the licence:**
>
> 1. **Always cite the DOI, never a mirror.** This PDF was retrieved from a third-party mirror because `oecd.org` blocks automated access. The *content* is identical and the licence permits redistribution — but a citation to a Lithuanian government website would look careless. Cite the OECD.
> 2. **Verify the instrument number.** The OECD Recommendation that embodies these Guidelines is **OECD/LEGAL/0414** (adopted 2015, **reinforced 3 May 2024**). Several secondary sources circulating online cite "OECD/LEGAL/0419" — that is **wrong**, and it refers to a different instrument. Check it before you commit it to Chapter 2.

## What does *not* go in here

Everything from the other 12 source categories in `09-Ethics-Access-and-Sharing.md`. In particular:

| Not here | Why | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| National statutes (PFM Acts, PBMA Act) | Government sites are usually **not** openly licensed for redistribution, even when the text is freely readable. Quote the sections you need at length — that is normal scholarly practice — and **link** to the source | Quote in the dissertation; link in the bibliography |
| Auditor General reports | Public documents tabled in Parliament, but not open-licensed for bulk redistribution. Quote findings with page and paragraph numbers | Quote; link |
| IMF Article IV reports | Copyrighted; quote briefly with full citation | Quote; link |
| Journal articles | Almost always paywalled. **Never** post a PDF anywhere | Full Harvard citation + DOI; quote a sentence or two |
| Your NFS app and any real shift report | Contains crew names and operational detail | `_LINK-ONLY/` — and the app needs **written employer permission** before it goes anywhere |

## Folder discipline

Keep two folders on your machine, and decide which one a file belongs in **the moment you download it**:

```
Christian-Capstone/
├── _CAN-SHARE/          ← OECD PDF, your own charts and CSVs, open-licence papers (CC BY, World Bank / IDB / CDB open access)
└── _LINK-ONLY/          ← statutes, AG reports, IMF reports, budget documents, journal PDFs
```

When you submit, `_CAN-SHARE/` can go in as-is. `_LINK-ONLY/` contributes quotations and links only.

> **On your own work:** everything *you* produce — the PCGI scoring sheet, the Document Register, the Gap tables, the radar charts, the Transferability Matrix — is yours and goes in `_CAN-SHARE/`. **The one exception is the NFS app**, which is your employer's, not yours.
