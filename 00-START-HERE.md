## The three things that matter most

1. **The OECD benchmark has no chapter on consequences.** That is not a problem for you — it is your finding. See `02-OECD-2024-Benchmark-Map.md` Part B. It means the Consequence Gap is partly *designed into the standard*, not merely a Caribbean implementation failure. Handle this well and it is the most original thing in the dissertation.

2. **"No quote means no score."** Your manual is emphatic. Every one of the 84 scoring cells needs a verbatim quotation and a section number. The scoring sheet has columns for exactly this, plus a `Why_Not_One_Higher` column — fill that in for every cell. It is the difference between a dissertation and a spreadsheet.

3. **Separate what the rules *require* from what actually *happens*.** The Framework corpus and the Evidence corpus must never be mixed in one column, one table or one argument. Almost every markable insight in this project lives in the **gap between the two**.

## Before you go further — two things to decide

**A. The 21 indicators are not published anywhere.** Your manual names only D1, D2 and D5. `02-OECD-2024-Benchmark-Map.md` Part D proposes a full set, each indicator tied to a specific citable OECD provision. Adopt it, adapt it, or replace it — but **lock it before you start scoring** and justify it explicitly in Chapter 3. Changing indicators halfway invalidates every score you have taken.

**B. Decide now whether the shift-report app is in or out.** It is a genuine working example of private-sector-style performance-control discipline inside a Bahamian SOE — which is squarely on your topic. But using your own workplace artifact as evidence raises **access, confidentiality and insider-researcher** questions that belong in the ethics form, not in Chapter 4. `08-Insider-Practice-Note-WITHHELD.md` sets out both the opportunity and the risk, including a sanitised alternative that avoids them.
