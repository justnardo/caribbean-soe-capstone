# 08 — Insider Practice Note *(withheld from the public copy)*

This numbered slot holds the insider practice note: a short case write-up examining a **live internal operational-control system at the author's own employer**, a state-owned enterprise.

It is in the private working folder only.

---

## Why it is not published here

The pack's own ethics and sharing section (`09-Ethics-Access-and-Sharing.md`, row 12) rules that this artifact may be reproduced **only with written employer permission and after redaction** of operational specifics and personal data.

Publishing it openly before that permission exists would breach exactly the condition the pack sets out — and it is the author's decision to make, not a technical one. So it is held back.

## Why it still matters to the project

It is the **strongest single item** in the practice shortlist (`07-Private-Sector-Practices.md`, practice **P-09**).

Every other candidate practice rests on a *literature* rationale — someone argued it should work. P-09 rests on **implementation evidence**: the discipline is already running, in production, inside a Caribbean SOE. For a project whose central question is whether private-sector performance discipline can actually survive contact with small-state SOE governance, that is a materially different class of evidence.

Nothing about the *practice* is confidential. The confidentiality attaches to the **employer** and to the **operational specifics of the live system** — not to the idea of role-based escalation, mandatory exception capture, or block-on-send validation. Those can be described, assessed and recommended freely.

## The route to using it

1. **Write the ethics section first**, before requesting anything. Say what you want, why, how it will be stored, and how you will redact it.
2. **Ask in writing** for permission to describe the system at a *pattern* level, naming no employer and no individual.
3. **Use the sanitised alternative** described in the private note if permission is refused or delayed — the practice shortlist survives it, because P-09 is scored on fit and feasibility, not on the employer's identity.
4. **Never attach a real submitted report.** They contain named individuals.

> **A note on method.** Using your own workplace as a research site makes you an *insider researcher*. That is not a weakness — it gives you access and understanding that no external researcher would get — but it places you under a specific obligation: to show, explicitly, that your position did not shape how you scored the evidence. State the mitigation in the ethics form and return to it in the limitations chapter.
