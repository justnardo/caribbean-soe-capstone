## Section 1 — Principal–agent theory and the ownership problem

**Why you need it:** your entire research question is an agency problem. In a private firm the principal is identifiable (shareholders) and the agent's failure has a consequence (the share price, the board, the market for control). In an SOE the principal is **diffuse and remote** — the OECD says so itself, in the phrase that gives your dissertation its title:

> "…the **accountability for the performance of SOEs involves a complex chain of agents** (management, board, ownership entities, ministries, the government and the legislature), **without clearly and easily identifiable, or with remote, principals**…"

That single quotation is your theoretical foundation. Cite it in the first paragraph of Chapter 2 and the whole chapter has somewhere to go.

| Ref | Citation | Verification | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Jensen, M.C. and Meckling, W.H. (1976) 'Theory of the firm: Managerial behavior, agency costs and ownership structure', *Journal of Financial Economics*, 3(4), pp. 305–360. doi:10.1016/0304-405X(76)90026-X | ✅ Crossref confirmed | The founding statement. Agency costs, monitoring costs, bonding costs, residual loss — the vocabulary you will use throughout |
| **A2** | Fama, E.F. and Jensen, M.C. (1983) 'Separation of ownership and control', *The Journal of Law and Economics*, 26(2), pp. 301–325. doi:10.1086/467037 | ✅ Crossref confirmed | **Cite this for the "disciplining factors" argument.** Decision management vs decision control; the market for corporate control as the ultimate sanction. This is the theory the OECD itself invokes when it concedes that SOE managers are "protected from two disciplining factors… the possibility of takeover and the possibility of bankruptcy" |
| **A3** | Eisenhardt, K.M. (1989) 'Agency theory: An assessment and review', *Academy of Management Review*, 14(1), pp. 57–74. doi:10.2307/258191 | ✅ Crossref confirmed | Your methods/lit-review bridge. Also gives you the **positivist vs principal–agent** distinction and the crucial point that agency theory assumes **goal conflict and outcome measurability** — both of which fail in an SOE, which is exactly your case |
| **A4** | Shapiro, S.P. (2005) 'Agency theory', *Annual Review of Sociology*, 31, pp. 263–284 | 🔍 | Useful critique: agency theory's assumptions about rational self-interest travel badly across institutional contexts. Strengthens your **SIDS-adaptation** argument |

**The move to make in Chapter 2:** establish the private-sector agency model (A1, A2), then show that **every one of its disciplining mechanisms is either absent or attenuated in a Caribbean SOE** — no takeover (C6), no bankruptcy (C6), no share price, no comparator (C4), and a principal who is themselves politically exposed (C2). **You have then derived your research question from theory rather than asserted it.**

**The critical reading to develop (and the most original thing in your dissertation):**

The 2024 Guidelines contain **no dedicated chapter and no dedicated Guideline on consequences for underperformance.** A full-text search for *consequence, sanction, penalty, withholding, remedial, enforcement, dismissal* returns no such provision. Consequence-adjacent language exists only in the **non-binding Annotations**, and mostly concerns breaches of **law** rather than **performance** failure.

Two consequences follow, and both belong in Chapter 2:

1. **The Consequence Gap is partly designed into the standard.** Your P2 is not merely a story about Caribbean implementation failure — the benchmark itself is soft on consequences. Reporting this honestly makes your analysis stronger, not weaker, and it is the kind of critical reading that distinguishes a distinction-level dissertation.
2. **The Guidelines declare themselves "outcomes-based"** and concede that "**no one size fits all**." That sentence is the **licence** for your Chapter 5 recommendation. You are not proposing deviation from the benchmark; you are exercising the discretion the benchmark explicitly grants. Quote it in Chapter 5.

Both are set out with exact quotations in `02-OECD-2024-Benchmark-Map.md`.

| **E5** | Stone, D. (2012) 'Transfer and translation of policy', *Policy Studies*, 33(6), pp. 483–499 | 🔍 | **The "translation" turn** — policy changes as it moves. This is precisely what your C1–C6 constraints do to transferred private-sector practice. Strong for Chapter 5 |

> **How to use this section — a concrete paragraph plan for Chapter 5:**
>
> 1. Transfer is not a binary (copy / don't copy) — it is a continuum (E1).
> 2. Transfer is **selective and constrained** by the receiving jurisdiction (E4).
> 3. Practice **changes in transit** — translation, not transplantation (E5).
> 4. Non-state actors are a recognised transfer channel (E2).
> 5. **Therefore:** the appropriate form of transfer to a Caribbean SIDS is **hybridisation, not copying** — and your Transferability Matrix is a systematic method for deciding, practice by practice, where on that continuum each one belongs.
>
> That is a properly theorised Chapter 5, and it is where your dissertation earns marks that a purely descriptive one will not.

## Quality rules for your final reference list

1. **Every in-text citation must appear in the list; every list entry must be cited in the text.** Check mechanically, from a printout, with a highlighter. This is the single most common source of lost easy marks.
2. **One style throughout.** Abertay uses **Harvard**. Match the exact Abertay guide, including DOI formatting.
3. **Page numbers on every direct quotation.** Non-negotiable, and your manual's "no quote means no score" rule makes it doubly so.
4. **Verify every volume, issue and page range.** This is why the ✅ column exists. Errors here look like carelessness even when the thinking is excellent.
5. **Balance your sections.** If Section 6 (SIDS) collapses to two sources while Section 3 has fifteen, your literature review is unbalanced — and a marker will read that as a thinner understanding of the Caribbean context than of the theory. **Actively look for Caribbean material to rebalance.**
6. **Aim for 40–60 sources**, roughly: 10–12 theory · 8–10 OECD/framework · 8–10 state ownership · 6–8 NPM and performance · 6–8 policy transfer · **6–10 SIDS** · 6–8 methodology · 4–6 measurement. Your manual's 40–60 target is achievable within four weeks — but only if you start now.
