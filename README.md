# Caribbean SOE Governance — MBA Capstone Research Materials Pack

**Project:** *Consequences without owners? Transferring private-sector performance and accountability practices to state-owned enterprises in Caribbean Small Island Developing States*

A **materials pack** for a comparative multiple-case-study research project on why state-owned enterprises (SOEs) in Caribbean SIDS adopt the *form* of private-sector performance management but not the *consequences*.

**Cases:** The Bahamas · Jamaica · Barbados · Trinidad and Tobago
**Benchmark:** OECD *Guidelines on Corporate Governance of State-Owned Enterprises* (2024 revision)
**Method:** Comparative multiple-case study, mixed methods, secondary data only

---

## What this is — and what it isn't

This is **not a draft dissertation**. It is the raw material and the apparatus:

- the benchmark, mapped chapter by chapter
- the four national legal frameworks, located and verified
- the evidence sources — audit reports, committee reports, IMF surveillance — with working URLs
- a scoring architecture (the proposed **21-indicator PCGI**)
- a shortlist of transferable private-sector practices, pre-scored
- for every item, **a plain-language ruling on whether it can be shared, quoted, or only cited**

The argument, the writing and the judgements are the author's own.

## Two honest notes about this public copy

**1. One file has been withheld.** The original working folder contains a tenth numbered file — an insider practice note examining a live internal operational-control system at the author's own employer, an SOE. It is a strong piece of implementation evidence, but publishing it before written employer permission would breach the confidentiality conditions the pack itself sets out in `09-Ethics-Access-and-Sharing.md`. The slot is retained as `08-Insider-Practice-Note-WITHHELD.md` so the numbering and cross-references stay intact. It can be shared privately on request.

**2. This is a working research pack, not a finished document.** Several items are marked as *located but unverified*, and the gaps are recorded rather than papered over. Where a source could not be retrieved, that is stated with the reason.

### What was verified

Every URL marked ✅ was fetched and confirmed to respond. The Bahamas Auditor General report archive (16 years), the Trinidad and Tobago Auditor General report archive, the four national framework documents, the IMF country-report numbers and the three published SOE sampling frames were all checked directly.

## Verification status of sources

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ | Fetched and confirmed responding |
| 🟡 | Located; not yet independently confirmed |
| ⚠️ | Known access problem — and the workaround |
| — | Genuine gap, recorded as a finding rather than filled |

Two access patterns affect several sources and are **not** evidence that a document does not exist: some government sites return **403 to automated requests** (IMF downloads, the IDB, Bahamas government pages), and some pages are **JavaScript-rendered** (Barbados State Bodies, parts of the T&T Auditor General document library). Both resolve when opened in a normal browser.
